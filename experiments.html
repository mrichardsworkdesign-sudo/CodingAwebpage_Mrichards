Inspiration

01

Dots & Type

Type that doesn't sit still: letters built from points that react when you get close.

Website

The Coding Train: "Steering Behaviors"

Daniel Shiffman's coding challenge turns a word into points that steer to their places and flee from the mouse. It's the core idea behind my particle text: every dot has a home, and it always finds its way back.

Visit site ↗
Artist

Zach Lieberman

The creator of openFrameworks and co-founder of the School for Poetic Computation, Lieberman calls what he builds "playful systems." His daily code sketches made me want my type to feel alive rather than animated.

Visit site ↗
Artist

Refik Anadol

Anadol turns huge collections of data into moving "data paintings" and sculptures. His work showed me how thousands of small points moving together can read as one form, which is what happens when the dots settle into a word.

Visit site ↗
02

Shapes & Play

Simple geometry, bold color, and the fun of knocking things around.

Website

Patatap

Made by Jono Brandel and Lullatone, Patatap sets off a sound and a burst of animated shapes with every key you press. It made me want shapes that react the instant you touch them.

Visit site ↗
Artist

Wassily Kandinsky

In a 1923 Bauhaus questionnaire, Kandinsky paired a yellow triangle, a red square and a blue circle, testing his belief that every basic shape has a color that belongs to it. My shapes use the same basic vocabulary: circles, squares and triangles.

Website

Bruno Simon

Bruno Simon's portfolio is a 3D world you explore by driving a car, full of physics you can mess with. It showed me that a portfolio can be played, not just scrolled.
